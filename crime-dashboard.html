why rates

Rates, not raw counts

Counting incidents ranks cities by population and tells you nothing. Everything in Crime Intelligence is per 100,000 residents, with the year-over-year change beside it, so a bad year reads differently from a bad decade. It was built for someone who wanted to look at the numbers himself instead of reading someone's summary of them, and grew from 15 cities to 43.

inside

Inside Crime Intelligence

01

A map that follows you

Colour-coded markers sized by population on a satellite map, following whichever category you pick.

02

The composite score

FBI rates 40%, dispatch density 20%, NeighborhoodScout 15%, CAP Index 15%, OpenCrime and SafeWise 10%. Modeled figures are labelled as modeled.

03

Rank and compare

Search, sort any column, filter by size or state, open a city for its detail against national averages.

04

Opens anywhere

One file: double-click it from a USB stick, an email or a desktop folder. Only the map tiles need a connection.

caveats it states out loud

Voluntary reporting: a city that looks safe may simply have reported less. Definition drift: category boundaries shift between standards. City limits aren't metros. Small denominators: a handful of incidents swings a small city's rate, so rank order at the bottom is noise.

built on

Leaflet for the map, FBI city figures baked in as a JSON literal, all state in memory. No fetch, no API key to expire, no build.

honest scope

Figures are projected to 2025 with the FBI's national trend, not measured. A dashboard that hides its seams makes people confident about things they shouldn't be.

Look at the numbers yourself. Then look at the caveats.
