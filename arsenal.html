why this exists

A garage tool, built not bought

the problem

Working on a specific car means constantly cross-referencing a workshop manual's text against a mental model of where everything actually sits — which mount, which coupling, which sensor is under which panel. That translation is where mistakes happen. Mechanic's Arsenal replaces the mental model with an actual one: a real 3D layout of a 2008 Volvo XC70 you can rotate, X-ray, and pull apart part by part.

what it does
  • Scan — search or click any of 126 cataloged parts across engine, driveline, brakes, suspension, cooling, climate, audio, safety, and body systems; each one carries a real description and where it physically lives.
  • X-ray & explode — fade the body to see through it, or blow every part apart along its own axis to see how the assembly actually stacks.
  • Arsenal — the tool catalog for the job: what you'd reach for to actually get a given part off the car.
  • Paint & cabin — swap exterior color and interior trim to match the real car being worked on.
  • Scene & camera — six lighting environments (neon district, studio, sunset, overcast, night city, garage) and full camera control, because a part looks different in shop light than in a photo.
  • Data — reference specs pulled from the factory workshop manual: torque values, tire and wheel-bolt specs, ignition data, and more, each one citing its source.
built on

Three.js, hand-modeled geometry for the mechanical layout, and a from-scratch HUD — no car-configurator template, no off-the-shelf 3D asset pack. The whole thing runs client-side: one HTML file, no server, no build step.

honest scope

Body, cabin, lamp, and chassis geometry are modeled from the real XC70. Mechanical parts are placeholder shapes placed at their correct real-world location to show layout and relationships, not exact OEM geometry — and every spec on the Data tab is reference only. Confirm torque values and procedures against the factory manual for your specific VIN before turning a wrench.

Built for the garage, not the showroom.
