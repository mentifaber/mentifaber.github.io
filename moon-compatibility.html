the idea

Two moons, overlaid

A compatibility reading built from the one thing two birthdays share with the sky: the phase of the moon. Each date's lunar age is placed on the 29.53-day synodic month, both moons are drawn and laid over each other, and the distance between them becomes the score.

what it does

Inside Moon Compatibility

01

Overlaid phases

Each moon drawn on its own canvas from its illumination fraction, terminator curve and all, then stacked so the difference is visible at a glance.

02

Per-date readout

Phase name, moon age in days, illumination, and the new moon that came before.

03

Cycle chart

The full illumination curve from new to full to new, both dates plotted on it, with a bracket measuring the gap.

04

The reading

Written for the pair of phases you land on: twins, neighbours, complements or opposites.

05

Date picker

Two dates, optional names, a swap button, and a copy-link button that saves the pair in the URL. Phase ages come from the mean synodic month anchored to a known new moon, so they are within about a day.

built on

Vanilla JS and the canvas API — no charting library, no astrology API. Lunar ages come from the synodic cycle, with the footer citing NASA RP 1349.

honest scope

The astronomy is real — phase ages on the synodic cycle, illumination from the moon's age. The compatibility is poetry, and it says so by being beautiful rather than by pretending to be a test. It was made for one particular pair, whose dates are still the default. Now it takes any two dates, and the link remembers them.

Two growing lights, less than a day apart.
