The premise

The honest part is the unknowns

Every scanner app gives a number. The number is only meaningful if you know how much of the label it actually understood. Tap any ingredient above to see why it landed where it did — including the ones marked unclassified, which is the whole point of the rebuild.

Classified

Matched against the ingredient table with a rating and a stated reason. These are the ones the score can honestly stand on.

Unclassified

Present on the label, absent from the table. Surfaced explicitly rather than silently treated as neutral — which is how a mystery additive becomes a good score.

AI classify

An escape hatch for the unclassified ones: send the raw ingredient string to a model and get a provisional read, clearly marked as provisional.

Coverage number

What fraction of the label was understood. A 78/100 across four of eleven ingredients is a different claim than 78/100 across eleven of eleven.

Open bugs

Two things that are still wrong

Both live in findAlternatives(), and both trace back to the same root cause: trusting that upstream data is complete.

Bug 1 — self-substitution

The recommended-substitute feature sometimes returns the scanned product as its own alternative. No identity check on the candidate set before ranking.

Bug 2 — truncated data

Open Food Facts returns truncated ingredient lists for some products. A short list looks like a clean product, so substitute scoring ranks incomplete entries above complete ones.

Why they're linked

Both are the scorer treating absence as good news. The same instinct the app exists to argue against, reproduced inside the app.

The shape of the fix

Exclude the source barcode from the candidate pool, and gate any substitute below a coverage floor out of the ranking entirely.

The scanner card above demonstrates the self-substitution bug on the last sample — it is showing you the real failure rather than a cleaned-up version of it.

Moss green and a worm

The rebrand from "Yuka But Honest" mattered more than a rename usually does. The old name defined the product as a critique of another product.

Palette

Earthy moss green throughout — soil, lichen, rust. Nothing about it reads as a clinical health app, because that framing implies an authority the data doesn't support.

Logo

A custom SVG worm, drawn as a single stroke path. It's above this page's title — same file, no raster fallback.

Name

A crawler that goes through what's underneath. Also literally a worm in soil, which is the joke.

Data source

Open Food Facts — open, community-maintained, and uneven in exactly the ways the app now surfaces instead of hiding.
