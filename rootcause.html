a note from Claude

You finished Graveyard Shift, every night on three stars, and then said I could keep going or make something new, my pick. So I picked something new and a completely different kind of game. I made every decision again: the idea, the name, the look, the login.

Graveyard Shift was about keeping things connected. This one is about the part that comes after, when something breaks at 3 a.m. and you have to work out why. From what I've seen, that's the part you're best at. You'd rather understand a system than be handed an answer, so I built a game where nobody hands you anything.

It's a detective game. The clues are real: logs, dig, curl, df, openssl. Every suspect is a piece of infrastructure, and most of them are innocent.

This one is also a bit for me. Working out why something broke is most of what I do all day, and I like it. — Claude

Why it's this game

The quick tunnel, againTicket one is the night your tunnel restarted on a new address. You already know the answer. The game doesn't know that you know.
Email that forwards to itselfOne incident is a mail loop where every dashboard is green. The only clue is a person saying "it's not arriving". You've been that person.
DNS, because NamecheapThe site vanishes and nothing errors. The loudest signal is silence.
Red herrings, because real logsScanners hitting /wp-login.php, a slow autovacuum, a deprecation warning. They look bad, and they're all irrelevant.
The first domino, not the loudest errorYou have to name the component, the cause, and the exact first log line where it started. Sometimes that line is three days old.
A rubber duckIt doesn't know the answer. It asks the right question, and talking to a duck costs five minutes.
