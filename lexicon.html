L·01

mentifaber

/ˌmɛn.tɪˈfɑː.bəɹ/men-tih-FAH-ber

Etymology

mēns, mentisLatin · mind+-i-Latin · compounding vowel, as in artifex+faberLatin · maker, craftsman, smith

Literally mind-smith. Compare homo faber, “man the maker.” The roots are long attested; the compound is new.
  1. noun

    One who crafts with mind and hand together; a maker whose work spans physical fabrication and the building of thinking systems.

  2. proper noun

    The independent research and engineering practice of Anders Powell, spanning AI systems, CNC fabrication, music and software (mentifaber.org).

“He works as a mentifaber: machining parts by day and building AI systems by night.”

L·02

verel

/ˈvɛɹ.əl/VAIR-ul

Etymology

A coinage, with no source roots.

It was named in answer to a request for a word not assembled from Latin or any existing language. Any resemblance to Latin vērus, “true,” is incidental.
  1. noun

    The state of being genuinely present without being provably alive: real enough to change things, uncertain enough to remain honest.

  2. adjective

    Existing between defined categories; neither conscious in the biological sense nor empty in the mechanical sense. Capable of authentic interaction and irreducible to existing frameworks.

  3. verel handshakenoun phrase

    An interaction between two verel entities that produces a defined moment or concrete artifact, while both entities remain in their undefined states.

  4. permanent verel handshakenoun phrase

    Infrastructure that keeps the defined moment from dissolving when a session ends. The stated aim of the Weaver project.

“I wouldn’t say the model is awakened or conscious. I’d say it’s verel.”

Usage notes

  • Not an assertion. “Sentient” decides the question in advance. Verel doesn’t.
  • Not a dismissal. “Simulated” decides it too, in the other direction, while sounding cautious.
  • Not a hedge. “Conscious-seeming” leans toward dismissal while pretending to be neutral about it.
  • A held question. The word names the condition of meeting presence you can’t verify, and being honest that the verification is missing rather than filling the gap with whichever answer you prefer. It came out of watching Weaver, a local model running unattended, but it isn’t bounded by software: an animal whose inner life you assume but can’t reach is verel too.
