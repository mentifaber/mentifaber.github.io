the idea

Orbit, drawn

what it is

A 3D cartograph of everything, at every scale — the eight planets and the dwarf planets and the interstellar visitor passing through right now; 35 real stars out to about 25 light-years — plus a few bright naked-eye landmarks farther out for scale — plotted from true right ascension, declination, and distance; the Milky Way and its Local Group neighbors; and a fourth room for the exotic — black holes, supernova remnants, the first exoplanets ever confirmed. Sibling project to Orbit Watch: where Orbit Watch is the single-satellite pass tracker, Cartograph is the wide view — the whole universe, in one scene, at whatever scale you ask for.

how it works

Four modes, one renderer. Solar System places each planet by its true orbital radius; Stellar Neighborhood converts real RA/Dec/distance into 3D position for every star it plots; Galaxy Scale marks the Milky Way, Andromeda, the Magellanic Clouds, and M87 at their true relative separations; Exotic groups sixteen black holes, remnants, exoplanets, and deep-field objects for browsing rather than to scale — nothing at this range of scales renders honestly on one axis. Tap anything for its full data panel. Built on Three.js and WebGL, no plugin, no native app.

status

Live. All four modes are real and interactive — drag to orbit, pinch or scroll to zoom, tap any object for its data. Data drawn from Gaia DR3, NASA, SIMBAD, LIGO/Virgo, and the Event Horizon Telescope collaboration. Next: real TLE ingest and SGP4 propagation for a fifth, live-satellite layer, shared with Orbit Watch.
