01Why it exists

A network that models feeling

Not classification: nodes carrying valence, propagating it and settling into configurations nobody specified in advance. Whether that resembles emotion or merely borrows its vocabulary is the open question. Moodfield puts a biological network, a psilocybin brain, an inconsistent AI and a consistent AI side by side, then merges them.

02Parts list

  1. v1 · two networksA six-region biological network beside a layered artificial one with dead units and dropout. Inject a stimulus, or glitch the AI and watch coherence fall.
  2. v2 · four networksAdds a psilocybin brain with whole-network waves and a consistent AI beside the inconsistent one, then merges all four into quadrants.
  3. v3 · mechanismsInhibitory neurons, theta and alpha oscillators, a thalamic gate that opens under psilocybin, mode collapse in the bad AI and residual paths in the good one.
  4. Cross-talkIn v3's merged view, dashed links carry feedback from biology to AI and an entropy channel from the psilocybin brain into the inconsistent AI.

03Construction

The earlier versions

They were kept, not overwritten, because the thing being studied is how the model changes. Open v1 or v2; they're the real earlier files. The live view below is v3.

The protocol

A Universal Consciousness Protocol: less a claim about consciousness than a specification for what evidence would have to look like. Captured states are frames pulled from the run, not prose written afterwards.

04Tolerances

None of it demonstrates consciousness. A system built to produce something that looks like inner state will produce something that looks like inner state. A simulation of a thing is evidence about the simulation. See Verel.
