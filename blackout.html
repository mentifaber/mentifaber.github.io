Blackout Kit

Get this ready while you still have a connection. Once it's ready, this page, the apps below and the world map open with no network: messaging over mesh radio and ham radio, a satellite tracker, your own documents, where you are, and plain guides for water, food, heat and power.

1 · Get ready

Checking…

    This keeps each app in this browser, about 10 MB in all. Add this page to your home screen too (your browser's Share or menu: "Add to Home Screen") so it opens like an app. Do it on every phone in the house.

    2 · Where am I

    Phones find themselves by GPS, which keeps working when the network doesn't. The map is drawn by this site from its own data, so it works offline too.

    3 · What to do

    Emergency numbers
    • 911 in the US and Canada, 112 across Europe and from most mobile phones anywhere, 999 in the UK.
    • When calls won't connect, try a text: texts often get through when the network is too busy for calls. In the US you can text 911 where it's supported.
    Safe water
    • Boil clear water at a rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft / 2,000 m). Let it cool.
    • No way to boil: unscented household bleach. For 6% bleach, 8 drops per gallon (about 4 L); for 8.25% bleach, 6 drops. Stir, wait 30 minutes. It should smell faintly of chlorine.
    • Cloudy water: let it settle and filter it through a clean cloth first, then treat it.

    From the US CDC's guidance on making water safe in an emergency.

    Food in a dead fridge
    • Keep the doors shut. A closed fridge keeps food cold for about 4 hours; a full freezer about 48 hours (24 if half full).
    • Throw out meat, dairy, eggs and leftovers that have been above 40°F (4°C) for more than 2 hours. When in doubt, throw it out.

    From the USDA's food safety guidance for power outages.

    Heat, generators and carbon monoxide
    • Never run a generator, grill, camp stove or charcoal indoors, in a garage or a tent, even with the door open.
    • Keep a generator at least 20 feet (6 m) from doors, windows and vents.
    • Headache, dizziness or sleepiness that several people share can be carbon monoxide: get everyone into fresh air and call for help.

    From the US CDC's carbon monoxide guidance.

    Keep the phone alive
    • Turn on low power mode, turn the screen brightness down, and switch on airplane mode when you don't need a signal.
    • A car charges a phone; run the engine outdoors only, never in a closed garage.
    • Agree a check-in time with family so phones can stay off in between.
    Radio
    • US weather and emergency alerts (NOAA Weather Radio) broadcast between 162.400 and 162.550 MHz; any weather radio receives them.
    • FRS walkie-talkies need no licence in the US. Channel 1 is 462.5625 MHz.
    • Ham radio's 2-metre calling frequency is 146.520 MHz FM. Anyone may listen; transmitting needs a licence, except in a real emergency.
    • Hopline and Skipline below message over mesh and HF radio when the internet is gone.

    General information, not a substitute for local emergency services. Follow the instructions of your local authorities.
